### Supplementary figures for results section: How does evolving permissiveness impact symbiotic evolution?

[Supplementary Figure 2.1](supplemental_figures/paper_results_2/evo_perm_behavior_compressed.pdf)
Evolved symbiont behavior across permissiveness mutation effect sizes. End-of-experiment replicate classification count under regimes of evolving permissiveness in experiment 5 (a) and experiment 6 (b). In experiment 5, we vary vertical transmission (VT) rate; in experiment 6 we vary tag mutation (TM) rate. Hosts began the experiments with permissiveness 0.375. Organisms start the experiment with all-0 tags. Organisms start as a mix of mutualists and parasites in experiment 5 and all parasites in experiment 6. Replicates are marked as exclusively parasitic if all symbionts have interaction value $<-0.2$ and exclusively mutualistic if all symbionts have interaction value $\geq0.2$.

[Supplementary Figure 2.2](supplemental_figures/paper_results_2/mut_vs_para_counts.pdf)
Mean final permissiveness across permissiveness mutation effect sizes. Count of replicates with mean final host permissiveness values at differing levels of permissiveness mutation size in experiment 5 (a) and experiment 6 (b). In experiment 5, we vary vertical transmission (VT) rate; in experiment 6 we vary tag mutation (TM) rate. Mean final permissiveness is collected from the last update in which a surviving symbiont exists; for non-extinct symbiont populations, this update is the last of the experiment. We record mean final permissiveness because permissiveness undergoes drift after symbionts go extinct. Hosts began the experiments with permissiveness 0.375. Organisms start the experiment with all-0 tags. Organisms start as a mix of mutualists and parasites in experiment 5 and all parasites in experiment 6. 

[Supplementary Figure 2.3](supplemental_figures/paper_results_2/mut_vs_para_counts.pdf)
Count of mutualists (a) and parasites (b) under low-mutation mutualism and high-mutation coexistence. Count of mutualists increases with permissiveness mutation size; coexistence consistently has a high count of mutualists. 


### Supplementary figures for results section: How does partner choice structure affect symbiont evolution?


[Supplementary Figure 3.1](supplemental_figures/paper_results_3/raw_vs_cor_metric_behaviors.pdf)
Hamming evolves less extinction and more exclusive mutualism than raw Hamming. Hamming compensates for raw Hamming's exclusionary nature. Counts of replicates are pooled across experiments 7 \& 8; we ran 5,130 total replicates for each metric. Streak and Hash are normalized by construction; adding a normalization correction had no appreciable affect.

[Supplementary Figure 3.2](supplemental_figures/paper_results_3/ham_sub_hash_full.pdf)
Hamming metric and Hash metric evolve different levels of exclusive mutualism, exclusive parasitism, and symbiont extinction from each other. Each cell represents the count of Hamming replicates that evolve a behavior - the count of Hash replicates which evolve a behavior. Zeroes are discarded. 

[Supplementary Figure 3.3](supplemental_figures/paper_results_3/ham_sub_streak_full.pdf)
Hamming metric and Streak metric evolve similar levels of each behavior across treatments. Each cell represents the count of Hamming replicates that evolve a behavior - the count of Streak replicates which evolve a behavior. Zeroes are discarded. 

[Supplementary Figure 3.4](supplemental_figures/paper_results_3/metrics_tagdist_exp78.pdf)
Symbionts more closely track hosts under stable partner choice criteria. Tighter host--symbiont correspondence arises when partner suitability can be tuned gradually by small-effect mutations (Hamming metric), compared to control where mutation randomizes partner choice criteria (Hash metric). We collected tag distance at the end of each experiment. Per-treatment means were collected across all replicates with surviving symbionts at the end of the experiments. Only treatments with at least 15 surviving Hamming replicates and 15 surviving Hash replicates were included in the analysis. We excluded tag mutation 0.Annotations indicate significance (***: p<0.0001) under Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Bonferroni correction for 5 comparisons.
